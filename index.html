Facilitation tools that respect the room.

Four tools a scrum master actually opens during the week — a retro board, a standup facilitator, a capacity forecaster and an anonymous team health survey. They run entirely inside your browser. There is no account to create, no workspace to invite people to, and no server that could ever be asked to hand over what your team said.

Run the whole retro in one tab: collect in silence, reveal, dot-vote, then leave with owned actions.

Why it is built this way

Privacy is a facilitation feature

People self-censor when they suspect a tool remembers. Nothing here is transmitted, so that suspicion has nowhere to land. Your data lives in this browser until you export or delete it.

Never measure individuals

Turn timers help a facilitator keep a meeting inside its box and are never stored per person. Blockers aggregate by category. Nothing here can be turned into a performance report, by design.

Ranges, not single numbers

The capacity planner refuses to produce one figure, because one figure becomes a promise. It gives you a forecast with a confidence attached and expects you to say so out loud.

Your data is portable

Every tool exports JSON that imports into any other copy, and Markdown that pastes into a ticket or a wiki. Nothing is trapped, so nothing needs a migration plan.

Your data

A backup is a single JSON file covering every tool. Restoring merges it into what is already here — useful for moving between machines, or for handing a team's history to whoever takes over facilitation. Clearing your browser's site data does the same thing as the erase button, which is worth knowing before you rely on this for anything you cannot lose.
